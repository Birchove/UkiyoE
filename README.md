昔太史公究天人之际,通古今之变,成一家之言,而《史记》出焉。然其所记者,王侯将相、礼乐征伐;至于市井之谈、里巷之笑、一人一事之颦笑,则史不之及。欲知一代之风土人情,往往不于正史之篇章,而于笔记之字里:《世说》之玄远,《笑林》之诙诡,《梦华》之繁华——皆史之余,而亦史之真也。

孟元老南渡,追念汴京之盛,作《东京梦华录》,自比「梦游华胥之国」。盖向之寻常,即异日之怅惘;当时不经意者,即后人之所渴知也。今之世,言一瞬而万里,梗谚日生,舆论朝起而暮灭;一人一事之微,转瞬即逝。使无人录之,则数十年后,后人欲知吾辈之世,将何所凭?

浮世者,人世也,以其如浮沤之倏起倏灭;微言者,片语也,以其不烦而足以见义。本站之志:为斯世存其真,为后人留其言。不登庙堂之宏,而录民间之微;不为盖棺之论,而存一时之声。褒贬俱收,雅俗并采,俾览者得以观一时之风气,闻众人之谈,而自得其义焉。

### 所录何物

所录者,皆人间实有、实盛、实动人心之人与事。或一人之浮沉,或一事之喧议,或一俗之流变,或一笑之流传。

- **选材**:足以见一时之风气、动众人之谈、关世道之升降者。可录市井笑谈,不拒市井之谑;然不涉宏大政治,不录花边八卦,不收速生速朽之烂梗。
- **考据**:先求其全,后求其约。所据必寻多源,兼官方报道与民间声音(微博、知乎、B 站、抖音、社区、自媒体)——官方之言往往偏于克制,民间之声尤能得其全貌;事涉境外者,亦采境外主流媒体,而严拒小报标题、内容农场之流,可疑者孤证不采。存疑必标,绝不编造。

### 体例如何

每条目皆分两部,文风与立场严格有别:

- **记事** —— 以白话记录事件本身。客观克制,不挟情绪,不预设结论;若有争议,则并列各方说法,使读者自辨。篇幅务求充实,所据必寻多方。
- **微言** —— 仿「太史公曰」之笔,于记事之后附一段评语,以「微言曰」领起,统一用浅近文言(笔记体)。可有褒贬,可寓感慨,贵在微言大义,而不贵冗长。

**记事求其真,微言见其义。**

「浮世微言」是一部写给当下的民间史、笔记集。

史书向来记的是王侯将相、典章制度;而市井的闲谈、街巷的笑声、一个个普通人的悲欢,往往不入正史,却散落在笔记里——《世说新语》的玄远、《笑林广记》的诙谐、《东京梦华录》的繁华,正是「正史之外,而得其真」的那一路。

孟元老在南渡之后写下《东京梦华录》,只为把已经失去的汴京繁华记录下来——因为昔日的寻常,正是后来的怅惘;当时不经意的一切,正是后人所渴望了解的。今天,信息一日万里,新词热梗层出不穷,舆论朝生暮灭;一个人、一件事,转眼就被遗忘。若没有人把它们记下来,几十年后,后人想了解我们这个时代,又能凭据什么呢?

这就是本站的志趣:为这个时代留存它的真实,为后来的人留下它的话语。我们记的是民间,不是庙堂;存的是一时之声,不是盖棺之论。褒贬都收,雅俗都采,让读者得以看见一时之风气,听见众人之谈,而各有会心。

### 收录什么

所收录的,都是真实发生过、真实影响过人心的人与事。或一个人的起伏,或一件事的争议,或一种风俗的变迁,或一段笑谈的流传。

- **选材**:足以反映时代风貌、引起广泛讨论、关乎世道变迁的。可收市井笑话,不拒绝俚俗幽默;但不涉宏大政治,不记花边八卦,不收速生速朽的口水烂梗。
- **考据**:先求全面,再求简洁。必须多方采集来源,既看官方报道,也看民间声音(微博、知乎、B 站、抖音、社区、自媒体)——官方信息往往偏于克制,民间的声音才能拼出立体全貌;事涉外媒的,也参考境外主流媒体,但严格拒绝小报、标题党、内容农场,可疑来源不采用。有疑问一定标注,绝不编造。

**Whispers of the Floating World** (浮世微言, *Fúshì Wēiyán*) is a folk history of the present — a living collection of notes in the old Chinese *biji* tradition. The repository's name, *UkiyoE*, echoes *ukiyo-e* (浮世絵), the Japanese "pictures of the floating world" — only here, words stand in for pictures.

Official histories have always recorded kings, generals, rites, and institutions. The idle talk of the marketplace, the laughter of the lanes, the small joys and sorrows of ordinary people — these rarely make it into the official record. Yet they survive in the informal notebooks and miscellanies of the past: the worldly wit of *Shishuo Xinyu*, the jokes of *Xiaolin Guangji*, the nostalgic splendor of *Dongjing Menghua Lu*. These are the margins of history — and often its truth.

When the writer Meng Yuanlao fled south after the fall of the Northern Song capital, he set down *Dongjing Menghua Lu* simply to preserve the city he could never return to — for the ordinary life of one age is the longing of the next. Today, information travels in an instant; new slang and memes rise and die by the hour; a person, an event, is forgotten almost as soon as it appears. If no one records these things, what will people a generation from now have to hold on to, if they wish to understand our time?

That is the ambition of this site: to preserve the truth of this age, and to leave its words for those who come after. We record the folk, not the court; we keep the living voices of a moment, not the final verdicts of posterity. Praise and blame alike are gathered; the refined and the vulgar alike are admitted — so that a reader may glimpse the spirit of an age, overhear the talk of the crowd, and draw their own meaning.

### What we record

Every entry concerns something that genuinely happened — a person, an event, a custom, a joke — that genuinely left its mark on people's hearts.

- **Selection**: what reveals the temper of the times, stirs public conversation, or speaks to deeper shifts in the culture. We accept marketplace jokes and vulgar humour, but we draw a firm line: no grand politics, no celebrity gossip, no fleeting viral fads that die as quickly as they rise.
- **Sourcing**: breadth first, concision second. Every entry draws on multiple channels — official reports AND folk voices (Weibo, Zhihu, Bilibili, Douyin, online communities, independent media). Official accounts tend toward restraint; folk voices fill in the picture. Where a story reaches beyond China's borders, we consult major international outlets (BBC, Reuters, AP, etc.) — but we strictly exclude tabloids, clickbait, content farms, and propaganda outlets, whose contamination would distort the entire record. Doubtful sources are never used uncorroborated. We cite what we can verify; we mark what we cannot; we never fabricate.

### Structure

Every entry is built in two parts, with register and stance strictly separated:

- **Chronicle** (记事) — a plain, objective account of the event in modern vernacular Chinese. Restrained in tone, free of editorializing, it lays out the facts along with multiple perspectives where disputes exist, and lets the reader draw their own conclusions.
- **Commentary** (微言) — a short appraisal in classical Chinese, echoing the historian's "The Grand Historian remarks…". Here opinion is welcome: praise, blame, lament, irony. The form is brief, the register is classical, the aim is weight in few words.

In a phrase: **the chronicle seeks truth; the commentary finds meaning**. (The site itself is written in Chinese.)

### Four columns

| Column | Spirit |
|---|---|
| **奇人 · Figures** | Lives of the age, recording praise and blame alike, that the person may be seen whole. |
| **俗世 · Events** | Records of happenings and phenomena, setting out all sides, that the truth may be preserved. |
| **风物 · Customs** | Folk culture and internet wonders — seeing the age through the small things. |
| **笑语 · Humour** | Wit, jokes, and marketplace ribaldry — laughing without wounding, catching the pleasure of the thing. |

---

The floating world is like foam on water — here one moment, gone the next. If no one records it, who will pass it on? We make no claim to the grand historian's ambition, but we quietly hold on to one small hope: that a hundred years from now, someone might open these pages and glimpse what our age was like. That is enough.
